# Back to Your Best Bouldering at 39: A 16-Week Evidence-Based Training & Vegetarian Fat-Loss Plan

*Research report, November 2026. Not medical advice.*

**Bottom line:** For the first 4–8 weeks your fingers set the limit, not your muscles or your body weight. Pulley and tendon tolerance comes back more slowly than pulling strength after a 2-year layoff. Ramp climbing up gradually (1 → 2 → 3 sessions/week), use low-intensity daily finger loading before any heavy hangboarding, do two short strength/antagonist sessions a week, and run a moderate deficit (~0.5–0.6 kg/week) with ~140–160 g protein/day. Treat 62 kg as a direction to review at week 12, not a fixed deadline. Photo-only Cal AI logs can under-count meals by hundreds of calories, so verify intake with a food scale.

## TL;DR
- **Train fingers last-to-hard, climb first-to-more.** Weeks 1–4: low-load "Abrahangs"/no-hangs plus easy, high-mileage climbing. Weeks 5–8: moderate density hangs or repeaters. Weeks 9–14: MaxHangs on 20 mm at bodyweight. Weeks 4, 8 and 12 are lighter deload weeks; week 16 is a test week. Climb 1×/week now, 2×/week from week 5, a 3rd session only if fingers and recovery allow.
- **Eat ~1,850 kcal/day on average** (≈2,000 on climbing/hard days, ≈1,750 on rest days) **with 140–160 g protein in 4 feedings of ~35–40 g**, from dairy, eggs, soy, seitan, tempeh and legumes. Adjust using the weekly change in your 7-day average weight, not the app's TDEE guess. This pace (≈0.7–0.9% of bodyweight/week) is conservative-to-middle in the 0.5–1%/week evidence range.
- **Supplements worth it:** creatine monohydrate 3–5 g/day (strongest evidence; vegetarians gain most; expect +0.5–1 kg water), a protein powder for convenience, and B12. Vitamin D only if tested low. Gelatin/collagen + vitamin C is promising but thin evidence, and is **not vegetarian**. Track progress with a 20 mm max hang (% bodyweight), max pull-ups and a weighted pull-up.

## Key findings

**1. The tissue mismatch is the central risk of a comeback.** Muscle and nervous-system strength return within weeks; tendon and pulley adaptation is slow (Gilmore/Baar, *Sports Medicine – Open*: climbing-specific adaptations of flexor tendons, pulleys and volar plates develop over years, though turnover may be faster than once believed). Fingers and shoulders are the most frequent injury sites (Grønhaug et al. 2023, elite female IFSC climbers: 53.5% injured in 12 months, mostly shoulders 37.7% and fingers 34.4%). Quarmby et al.'s 2023 systematic review lists higher intensity, bouldering, reduced finger strength, crimp-grip use and previous injury as overuse risk factors. Bouldering + crimping + strength returning faster than tissue capacity is exactly the profile to manage.

**2. Low-intensity, high-frequency finger loading is a safe on-ramp, not a miracle.** In a retrospective study of 526 Crimpd users (Gilmore, Klimek, Abrahamsson & Baar, *Sports Medicine – Open* 2024), a 10-minute low-intensity protocol ("Abrahangs") improved grip strength as much as Max Hangs, and combining them was additive. Tyler Nelson (Camp4) notes the study measured strength, not tendon/pulley structure. Lattice: high-frequency low-intensity loading likely helps tendons; heavier loading adds the muscle/recruitment stimulus. **Use Abrahangs as the weeks 1–4 base, then layer in heavier hangs.**

**3. Hangboarding is appropriate now, at low intensity.** Lattice generally wants 6–12 months of consistent climbing before easier hangboard training; ~5 years of history qualifies. Keep it light, sessions under ~20 minutes, no strength sessions on consecutive days, and finger work at the *start* of a session, never the end.

**4. Strength training reduces climbing injuries (limited but real evidence).** The only RCT in climbers (Kozin et al. 2021, 84 male student climbers, closed-chain/eccentric/strength work 3–4×/week for a year): shoulder injuries fell from 3.182 to 0.5 per 1,000 athlete-exposures; a companion paper reported fewer elbow injuries. Moderate-quality single trial, but supports regular strength and shoulder work.

**5. Losing weight improves strength-to-weight, but under-fuelling is common and costly.** Keeping absolute finger strength from 72 → 62 kg raises % bodyweight by ~16% — roughly the gap between average male V4 and V6–V7 finger-strength ratios. But: Mora-Fernandez et al. 2024 (45 sport climbers) averaged energy availability 33 kcal/kg FFM/day, only 8.9% above the healthy 45; a Wrocław study (106 climbers) found 59% below 30 (low energy availability); in Grønhaug, disordered eating roughly doubled injury odds (not significant, p = 0.08).

**6. Your tracker probably under-counts.** In an NIH study (Hengist et al., 102 lab-prepared meals, NUTRITION 2026), four photo apps underestimated calories by about a third (250–345 kcal/meal). Cal AI under-counted most (avg 345 kcal/meal); all missed ~30 g fat. Weigh calorie-dense foods (oils, nuts, cheese, peanut butter, tahini) and correct entries.

## A. Returning-climber principles
- **First 2–3 sessions: mileage well below max.** Drop grade well below capability, then increase one or two grades per session. V0–V3 volume first, then V3–V4 attempts, then V4–V5 projecting by roughly weeks 5–8.
- **Change one variable at a time.** Add climbing frequency first, then hangboard intensity.
- **24-hour symptom check.** Finger/elbow discomfort ≤2/10 during loading and back to baseline next morning. If not, drop the next session's intensity.
- **Grip hierarchy.** Open hand and half crimp on ≥20 mm first. Full crimp, small edges, pockets and dynamic moves on crimps last.
- **Pulley injury:** typical return ~6–8 weeks for grade I–II, ~3 months for grade III (Schöffl), with taping afterward. A "pop", bruising or pain at the base of a finger → stop and get assessed.

## B. Fingerboard protocols
**Setup:** 20 mm edge, half crimp (index–ring bent ~90° at the middle joint, thumb off) and 4-finger open hand. Shoulders engaged, arms straight not locked. Bathroom scale under the feet (or a chair) to quantify feet-assisted load. Warm up 5–10 min first: jump rope, tendon glides, progressive hangs.

| Weeks | Protocol | Dose | Frequency | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1–4 | **Abrahangs / no-hangs** (feet on floor) | 10 hangs × ~10 s over ~10 min, alternating half crimp / open hand / 3-finger drag; effort ~2–4/10 (≈40–50% max) | 4–6×/week, ≥6 h from climbing | Study used ~40% 1RM / RPE 2 on 18–22 mm; very low risk |
| 5–8 | **Density hangs** (Nelson) or **7:3 repeaters** | Density: 3–5 hangs of 20–40 s near (not to) failure, 2–3 min rest, feet-assist to set load. Repeaters: 7 s on/3 s off × 6 = 1 set; 3 sets/grip, 3 min rest | 1–2×/week + Abrahangs other days | Builds tendon/muscle base |
| 9–14 | **MaxHangs** (Eva López) | 4–6 × 10 s, ~3 s in reserve, 3 min rest, 20 mm half crimp at bodyweight (assist if needed; add 5/8 kg dumbbells in a backpack once bodyweight is easy) | 1–2×/week, start of session | López's studies were on advanced climbers; use the structure, not the loads |
| 15–16 | Taper + test | 2–3 MaxHang sets, test in wk 16 | 1×/week | Abrahangs optional |

**Grip rules, first ~12 weeks:** no full crimp on the board, no two-finger pockets/monos, no edges <15 mm, no one-arm hangs, no campus board. Never to failure. Never at the end of a climbing session. Board time under 20 min.

## C. Exercise library (your equipment + park)
Two strength sessions/week, ~45 min, ~2 reps short of failure (RPE 7–8). Progress reps → difficulty → load. 5 + 8 kg dumbbells in a backpack for weighted work (up to 13 kg).

**Pulling**
- Scapular pull-ups: 2–3 × 8–10, 2 s hold. Always in warm-up.
- Pull-ups: 4–5 sets at ~50–70% of max reps (max 6 → 5 × 3–4). Progress to 5 × 5, then weighted 4 × 4–6 with 5 → 8 → 13 kg. Rest 2–3 min.
- Ring rows (feet elevated as you progress): 3 × 8–12, 3 s lowering.
- Lock-offs: 3–4 × 3–5 s holds at top, 90°, 120°, both arms. Only once 8+ clean pull-ups.
- Hanging core: knee raises → straight-leg raises → toes-to-bar, 3 × 6–10.
- Front lever: tuck → advanced tuck → one-leg, 4–6 × 5–10 s. Add tuck lever rows once holds reach 10 s.

**Core (mat)**
- Hollow-body hold 3 × 20–40 s → hollow rocks.
- Side plank 2–3 × 30–45 s each side.
- Ring or kneeling ab rollout 3 × 6–10.

**Push/antagonist**
- Push-ups on handles 3 × 10–20 → ring push-ups → deficit/archer.
- Parallel-bar dips: support holds 3 × 20 s, then 3 × 5–12 to a pain-free depth. Skip if the shoulder pinches.
- Pike push-ups 3 × 6–10.
- Dumbbell overhead press (8 kg, one arm) 3 × 10–15.

**Shoulder health (2–3×/week, 10 min)**
- External rotation: side-lying 5 kg or band "no-moneys", 2–3 × 12–15.
- Prone Y-T-W, bodyweight → 2–5 kg, 2 × 8 each.
- Ring face pulls / Y-raises 3 × 10–12.
- Scapular push-ups 2 × 10–15.

**Wrist/forearm antagonists**
- Reverse wrist curls (5 kg) 2–3 × 15–20.
- Pronation/supination holding the end of the 5 kg, 2 × 12 each.
- Rubber-band finger extensions 2 × 25.
- Tendon glides every warm-up.

**Legs, hips, mobility**
- Bulgarian split squats with dumbbells 3 × 8–10 → toward pistols.
- Hip mobility circuit, 10 min, 3×/week: frog stretch, pancake (30–60 s centre/left/right), 90/90 switches, deep squat hold, couch stretch.
- Barefoot jump rope 3–5 min; build up gradually (calves/Achilles need adaptation too).

**Park sessions**
- **A (pull):** jump rope 3 min → scap pulls → pull-ups 5 sets → ring rows 3 × 10 → tuck front lever 5 × 8 s → dips 3 sets → hollow 3 × 30 s → external rotations.
- **B (push/legs):** jump rope → ring push-ups 3 sets → pike push-ups 3 sets → DB overhead press 3 sets → lock-offs 3 × 3 holds → hanging leg raises 3 × 8 → split squats 3 × 10 → Y-T-W + reverse wrist curls → hip mobility.

## D. Climbing-day structure (~90 min)
1. **Warm-up (15–20 min):** jump rope 3 min; tendon glides; band external rotations; scap pulls; 8–12 easy problems V0 → V3 with silent feet; 2–3 harder problems or 2–3 recruitment pulls (3–5 s, feet on).
2. **Main block (40–50 min), one of:**
   - **Limit/projecting (from phase 2):** 3–5 problems at current max (V3–V5), 3–5 min rest, ≤5–6 quality attempts each. Stop when attempt quality drops.
   - **Volume/technique:** 4 × 4 on V2–V3 or pyramid (4 × V2, 3 × V3, 2 × V4, 1 × V5). Drills: silent feet, hover hands, down-climbing, flag-only problems, one avoided style (slab, compression, heel hooks).
3. **Flash block (10–15 min):** 3–4 new problems a grade below max; read and flash.
4. **Cool-down (10 min):** push-ups, wrist extensors, light stretching.

**Once-a-week climbing:** warm-up → 25 min projecting → 15 min flashing → 15 min volume. Avoid hard crimpy board climbing until phase 3.

## F. 16-week phased plan
| Phase | Weeks | Climbing | Fingers | Strength | Nutrition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Re-entry | 1–4 (wk 4 lighter) | 1×/wk, 2nd in wk 3–4 if pain-free | Abrahangs 4–6×/wk | Park A/B, rebuild reps | Start deficit; protein habit |
| 2. Build | 5–8 (wk 8 deload) | 2×/wk, V4 projecting | Density/repeaters 1–2×/wk | Pull-ups to 5 × 5; tuck lever | First diet check |
| 3. Strength | 9–12 (wk 12 deload) | 2–3×/wk, limit V4–V5 | MaxHangs 1–2×/wk | Weighted pull-ups 5–13 kg; lock-offs | Optional 1–2-wk diet break ~wk 12 |
| 4. Perform | 13–16 (wk 16 test) | 2–3×/wk, projecting/outdoor | MaxHangs 1×/wk, taper | Maintain (2 fewer sets) | Maintenance calories wk 16–17 |

Testing: week 0 (safe tests only), 8, 16. Minimum effective dose on a bad week: 1 climb + 3 × 10-min Abrahangs + 1 × 20-min park session.

## G. Nutrition
**Calories.** Mifflin-St Jeor BMR (male, 39, 166 cm, 72 kg) ≈ 1,570 kcal. Maintenance likely ~2,250–2,450 kcal with training 4–5×/week. A 0.5–0.6 kg/week loss needs ~550–650 kcal/day deficit:
- **~1,850 kcal/day average**: ~2,000 on climbing/strength days, ~1,750 on rest days.
- **Every 2 weeks:** 7-day average dropping <0.3 kg/week → cut 100–150 kcal (or add steps); >0.8 kg/week or performance/sleep suffering → add 150–200 kcal.
- **Energy availability:** ~55–57 kg FFM and ~300–400 kcal/day exercise → 1,850 kcal ≈ 26–28 kcal/kg FFM/day, already under the 30 low-EA threshold. **Don't go below ~1,750 average.** Prioritise carbs around climbing.

**Is 62 kg realistic?** 10 kg in 16 weeks = 0.625 kg/week (~0.87% → ~1% of bodyweight/week), the top edge of Helms et al.'s 0.5–1% range; slower (~0.5 kg/week) protects lean mass better. BMI at 62 kg ≈ 22.5. **Plan for ~64 kg at week 16, reassess at week 12**, continue toward 62 kg only if strength, sleep and mood hold. Creatine adds ~0.5–1 kg water: a scale stall in weeks 1–2 isn't failure.

**Protein.** Helms et al.: 2.3–3.1 g/kg FFM in a deficit (≈127–175 g). **Target 140–160 g/day in 4 feedings of 35–40 g.** Plant proteins have less leucine, so vegetarian meals should be at the upper end (30–40 g per meal ≈ leucine of ~25 g whey). Dairy, eggs and soy are the most leucine-dense.

**Vegetarian protein sources (approx.)**
- Skyr/Greek yogurt 0% (300 g) ≈ 30–33 g
- Low-fat cottage cheese/quark (250 g) ≈ 28–30 g
- Whey or casein (30 g) ≈ 24 g
- 3 eggs ≈ 18 g; egg whites 200 g ≈ 22 g
- Firm tofu 200 g ≈ 25–30 g
- Tempeh 150 g ≈ 28–30 g
- Seitan 100 g ≈ 20–25 g
- Cooked lentils/chickpeas 200 g ≈ 15–18 g
- Edamame 150 g ≈ 16–18 g
- Lentil/chickpea pasta 80 g dry ≈ 17–20 g

Vegan swaps: soy skyr/yogurt, soy or pea-rice protein, tofu scramble, seitan and tempeh; combine legumes and grains.

**Timing:** most carbs in the meal 2–3 h before climbing and the meal after; never train fasted in a deficit; protein within ~2 h after training.
**Hydration/sleep:** pale-yellow urine, +~500 ml per hour of climbing (more in heat). 7–9 h sleep; slipping sleep is often the first sign the deficit is too aggressive.

## K. Warning signs — back off
**Fingers/elbows/shoulders**
- Pain >2/10 during loading, or soreness still there next morning.
- Localised pain at the base or middle of a finger (pulleys), a "pop", swelling or bruising → stop climbing, see a hand/climbing physio.
- Inner/outer elbow pain when gripping → cut pulling ~50%, keep wrist-extensor work.
- Shoulder pinching on dips, overhead moves or lock-offs → remove the exercise, add rotator-cuff work.

**Overtraining / low energy availability**
- Performance down 2+ sessions in a row, benchmarks down >5%.
- Poor sleep, irritability, low motivation, elevated resting HR.
- Constant hunger, feeling cold, low libido, frequent illness, weight dropping >1%/week.
- Response: deload week; if persisting, 1–2 weeks at maintenance; GP visit (ferritin, B12, vitamin D).

## Caveats
Hangboard evidence is small (Abrahangs data retrospective, no tissue measures; López protocols on advanced climbers; one moderate-quality injury RCT). Grade/strength norms are averages, partly interpolated. Calorie/FFM figures are estimates — the weight trend is the ground truth. Prices change. Existing pain or a history of disordered eating warrants professional input first.
